# Research Results

Generated by `scripts/run_research.py`. Do not edit by hand.

**Question:** do these five classic strategies beat buy and hold once they are tuned without hindsight?

**Setup:** 30 walk-forward runs: 5 strategies on 6 ETFs, using daily data from 2007-01-01
to 2025-12-31. Parameters were chosen on 756 bars and traded on the next 252, with a commission of
0.1% and slippage of 5 bps per fill. Prices include dividends.

**Assets:** SPY (US large cap), QQQ (US technology), IWM (US small cap), EFA (Developed ex-US), TLT (Long Treasuries), GLD (Gold).

## Findings

- **Backtests overstated these strategies.** The mean Sharpe ratio fell from 0.86 in-sample to
  0.31 out-of-sample, and it fell in 29 of 30 runs.
- **Buy and hold was hard to beat.** Its mean Sharpe over the same periods was 0.60. Strategies
  had the higher Sharpe in 1 of 30 runs and the higher CAGR in 0.
- **The strategies did reduce risk.** 20 of 30 runs had a shallower maximum drawdown
  than buy and hold, helped by spending much of the time out of the market.
- **The benchmark matters as much as the correction.** 7 of 30 full-sample
  winners were significant against zero at the 95% level, and 0 were significant against
  buy and hold. The highest deflated Sharpe against buy and hold was 42.1%.

## Limitations of This Study

- Uninvested cash earns no interest here, which handicaps strategies that spend time out of the market.
- Sharpe ratios use a risk-free rate of zero for strategies and benchmark alike.
- Six assets over one period is a small sample, and these assets mostly rose over this period.
- A positive result here would not have established a tradable edge, and this negative result does not show
  that no variant of these ideas can work.
