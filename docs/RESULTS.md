# Research Results

Generated by `scripts/run_research.py`. Do not edit by hand.

**Question:** do these seven classic strategies beat buy and hold once they are tuned without hindsight?

**Setup:** 42 walk-forward runs: 7 strategies on 6 ETFs, using daily data from 2007-01-01
to 2025-12-31. Parameters were chosen on 756 bars and traded on the next 252, with a commission of
0.1% and slippage of 5 bps per fill. Prices include dividends, idle cash
earns the 13-week US Treasury bill yield, and Sharpe ratios are measured on returns above that yield.

**Assets:** SPY (US large cap), QQQ (US technology), IWM (US small cap), EFA (Developed ex-US), TLT (Long Treasuries), GLD (Gold).

## Findings

- **Backtests overstated these strategies.** The mean Sharpe ratio fell from 0.73 in-sample to
  0.20 out-of-sample, and it fell in 40 of 42 runs.
- **Buy and hold was hard to beat.** Its mean Sharpe over the same periods was 0.52. Strategies
  had the higher Sharpe in 1 of 42 runs and the higher CAGR in 1.
- **The strategies did reduce risk.** 33 of 42 runs had a shallower maximum drawdown
  than buy and hold, helped by spending much of the time out of the market.
- **The benchmark matters as much as the correction.** 8 of 42 full-sample
  winners were significant against zero at the 95% level, and 0 were significant against
  buy and hold. The highest deflated Sharpe against buy and hold was 54.4%.
- **Choosing the best parameters helped, but only a little.** The mean probability of backtest overfitting was 42%,
  where picking parameters at random would score about 50%. In 17 of 42 searches, the
  in-sample winner landed in the bottom half on unseen data in most splits of the history.

## Limitations of This Study

- Treasury bill yields were close to zero from 2009 to 2015 and again from 2020 to early 2022, so cash earned
  little for much of this period.
- Six assets over one period is a small sample, and these assets mostly rose over this period.
- Every strategy is long or flat. None can profit from a falling market.
- A positive result here would not have established a tradable edge, and this negative result does not show
  that no variant of these ideas can work.
